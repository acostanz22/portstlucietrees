Crane-assisted removal

Crane-Assisted Tree Removal in Port St. Lucie, FL

When a tree sits over a roof, a pool cage, or a narrow side yard, lowering every piece by rope is slow and sometimes unsafe. A crane lifts sections clear so the house stays intact.

Request an estimate Call 772-492-4436

When a crane belongs on the job

A crane is not a default for every backyard. It is the right tool when a large section has nowhere to land, when the trunk leans into a building, or when piecing everything by hand would leave the crew under hung wood too long. Port St. Lucie lots with screen enclosures and tight setbacks are common crane candidates.

When to call

  • The tree leans into or over a roof or pool cage.
  • There is no safe place to lower heavy wood by rope.
  • A previous storm left hung sections that need a controlled lift.
  • The customer wants the wood off the lot the same day the crane is on site.

How the work goes

  1. Confirm street and yard access for the crane truck.
  2. Rig the section, cut, and lift clear of the structure.
  3. Set wood where it can be cut and loaded without hitting the house.
  4. Finish cleanup and note stump grinding if it was priced.

What changes the price

  • Crane size and how long it has to stay on site.
  • Tree size and number of lifts.
  • Street permits or cone work if the truck blocks a lane.
  • Haul-away volume after the wood is on the ground.

Benefits of crane work next to buildings

The crane carries the weight. That reduces the chance a heavy trunk kicks into a roof or cage. It also shortens time spent working over fragile screens.

Problems crane removal solves

Hung limbs, trees with no drop zone, and trunks that would crush a fence if they spun. It does not replace calling the utility when the tree is in a power line.

Pricing notes

Crane days cost more than a climb-only job because the equipment and setup are part of the price. We only recommend it when the site needs it.

Process and access

We need firm ground or a street position for the outriggers. Soft lawn after rain can change the plan. Tell us about septic lids, irrigation, and neighbor fences before the truck arrives.

Questions about this job

Will the crane damage my yard?

Outriggers need a stable pad. We protect what we can and tell you where the truck will sit before work starts. Soft ground may force a street setup instead.

Can you use a crane near power lines?

Not while the tree is energizing a line. The utility makes the line safe first. After that, we can plan the lift.

Is every large tree a crane job?

No. Many trees come down with ropes. We recommend a crane when the geometry of the yard makes it the safer option.

Do I need to be home?

Someone should unlock gates and move cars. You do not need to stand outside while the saws run.

Talk through the tree

Call or send the property details. We will tell you if the job is a removal, a trim, or something that should wait for the utility.
